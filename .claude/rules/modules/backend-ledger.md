# backend: ledger

## Transaction amounts

A transaction is a header row of `transactions` (`app/models/accounting/transaction.py`): type, date, status, comment, `location_id` of an expense, `income_period` of an income, and the positive `base_currency_amount` that the list sorts and filters by and the summary sums, null for an expense not valued yet (**Unvalued expenses**), which the list sorts last either way. The check constraints `transaction_location_by_type` and `transaction_income_period_by_type` keep the two type-specific columns filled for their type only. The money it moves is two rows of `postings` (`app/models/accounting/posting.py`), each with an account, a signed `amount` in its own `currency` and a signed `base_amount`. The debit has the negative amounts and the credit the positive ones; their base amounts sum to zero, and each is the header's `base_currency_amount` in size. A credit may carry two more postings of an exchange difference (**Exchange differences**), whose amounts are 0. The processors write the header and its postings in one call (`transaction_crud.create_with_postings`).

| Type | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Expense | the money account, in its currency | the EXPENSE account, in the seller's currency |
| Income | the INCOME account, in the payer's currency | the money account, in its currency |
| Transfer | the source money account | the receiving money account |
| Adjustment | the EQUITY account, or the account for a decrease | the account for an increase, or the EQUITY account |

`Transaction` (`app/schemas/accounting/transaction.py`) returns the header and its `postings`, each with its whole account (`Posting` in `app/schemas/accounting/posting.py`). The relationship is ordered by amount with the zero amounts last, so the debit comes first, the credit second and an exchange difference after them, and an adjustment shows its EQUITY posting too.

The list (`transaction_crud.get_transactions`) reads the kinds of the requested `account_ids` and groups them: ASSET and LIABILITY form one money group, and every other kind its own. Each group is one condition on `postings.account_id`, so accounts of one group match either side of a transaction with OR, while the groups combine with AND: a card and a category list the card's expenses in that category, which the client's separate account, category and income source filters rely on. An id that is not one of the user's accounts lists nothing, even next to known ones, since dropping it would lift its kind's condition and widen the list. `location_ids` is one condition on `transactions.location_id`, so a transaction without a location never matches, and an id that is not one of the user's locations is dropped next to known ones: it matches no transaction of the user, and dropping it only narrows the list. The list breaks ties by `entry_order` in the direction of the first order; it follows entry and moves an edited transaction to the end of its date (**Recalculation**), so the four orders of the dictionary, which name one field each, keep a day's transactions in the order the ledger walks them. Without `date_from` the list has no lower bound, so the client pages through the whole history. `TransactionType` with `TRANSACTION_TYPE_LABELS` and the four sort orders of `TRANSACTION_ORDER_LABELS`, keyed by their `orders` query value, are served by `GET /dictionaries/accounting` as `transaction_types` and `transaction_orders`.

## Movements

`POST /accounting/transactions` takes one body, `MovementRequest`, which moves money from `from_account_id` to `to_account_id`; the two must differ. The request carries no type: `transaction_service.create_transaction` reads both accounts of the user, without a lock, since a kind never changes, and `factory.get_movement_processor` derives the type from their kinds (`MOVEMENT_TYPES`). A money account to an EXPENSE account is an expense, an INCOME account to a money account is an income, and a money account to a money account is a transfer. Any other pair, EQUITY included, answers 422 `MOVEMENT_NOT_ALLOWED`. An account the user does not have answers 404 `ENTITY_NOT_FOUND`, since its kind cannot be read. The processor then locks and checks the accounts as before.

The fields that belong to one type are optional in the body and checked by the processor (`TransactionProcessor._validate_fields`): an expense needs `location_id` and `destination_amount` and refuses `income_period`, an income needs `income_period` and `destination_amount` and refuses `location_id`, and a transfer refuses both. A missing one answers 422 `FIELD_REQUIRED` and a refused one 422 `FIELD_NOT_ALLOWED`, both naming the field in the message. An expense or a transfer that sends `base_currency_amount` answers 422 `BASE_CURRENCY_AMOUNT_NOT_ALLOWED`, because both derive it from the debited account's rate. `income_period` is moved to the first of its month.

`PUT /accounting/transactions/{transaction_id}` (`transaction_service.update_transaction`) takes the same body and picks the processor the same way, then `TransactionProcessor.update` replaces the transaction in place, keeping its id, so the client's link to it stays valid. It edits a movement only: a balance adjustment has its own body and endpoint (**Balance adjustments**), which run the same `update`. The type stays the transaction's own, and accounts whose kinds give another type answer 422 `TRANSACTION_TYPE_CHANGE_NOT_ALLOWED`, as a movement sent for an adjustment does; changing the type is a delete and a new transaction. `update` reverses the old postings on the cached balances, deletes all of them, the exchange differences included, and recalculates their money accounts from the old date, so an expense that the old postings valued waits again. Only then does `_prepare_transaction` run, on the ledger as it is without the transaction, as a create would. It then writes the header and the new postings (`transaction_crud.update_with_postings`), which also gives the transaction a new `entry_order`, applies them and recalculates their money accounts from the new date. The edited transaction thus comes last on its date, as a new one does, and the debit valued at the end of that day is the one the walk sees.

What source and destination mean for a request depends on the transaction type:

- **Expense**: `source_*` is the amount withdrawn from the account, and `destination_*` is the amount in the seller's or recipient's currency.
- **Income**: `source_*` is the incoming amount in its original currency, which may be any currency, and `destination_*` is the amount credited to the receiving account. When `destination_currency` is the base currency, `base_currency_amount` is `destination_amount`, the amount credited; otherwise, when `source_currency` is, it is `source_amount`. A request that sends `base_currency_amount` in either case answers 422 `BASE_CURRENCY_AMOUNT_NOT_ALLOWED`, even with the derived value, as an adjustment does. When neither currency is the base one, the request carries `base_currency_amount`, and an income without it answers 422 `BASE_CURRENCY_AMOUNT_REQUIRED`.
- **Transfer**: `source_*` is the withdrawal from one account, and `destination_*` is the deposit to the other account, which must be a different account. When both currencies are the same, `destination_amount` may be omitted and is set to `source_amount`; a different value answers 422 `TRANSFER_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`. A transfer into the base currency from another one derives `destination_amount` as the base amount of the debit, and a request that sends `destination_amount` answers 422 `DESTINATION_AMOUNT_NOT_ALLOWED`, even with the derived value.
- **Adjustment**: both postings carry the size of the request's signed `amount`, in the account currency.

## Postings

A money account (ASSET or LIABILITY) caches the sums of the postings of its active transactions: `balance` of their amounts and `base_balance` of their base amounts. The processor changes the cache of every money account a transaction posts to in the same database transaction (`account_crud.add_to_balance`), and deleting a transaction marks its header DELETED and subtracts its postings again. The other kinds keep a zero cache, since their postings are in several currencies.

An account's rate is never stored. `utils.get_base_currency_rate` derives it as `balance / base_balance`, how many units of the account currency one unit of the base currency buys, rounded to 10 decimal places, and 0 when either sum is 0. The account response shows it as `base_currency_rate` and the base value as `base_currency_balance`, `base_balance` rounded to cents.

A credit brings its own base amount: the income's, or the debit's on the other side of a transfer. A debit is valued at the account's average rate by one pure calculator, `ledger_service.get_base_amount`: the amount itself for an account in the base currency, all of `base_balance` when the debit empties the account (the amount equals the absolute balance), and otherwise `amount * base_balance / balance`, rounded to cents and kept at 0.01 at least, since an amount must be positive. An account outside the base currency has no rate (`ledger_service.has_base_currency_rate`) with a zero balance or base value, or the two of opposite signs, which a deletion can leave. An expense from such an account is stored unvalued (**Unvalued expenses**), while a transfer from it answers 403 `NO_ACCOUNT_BASE_CURRENCY_RATE`, since a transfer into the base currency derives its amount from the base amount. A new debit is valued and checked at the sums on its date, not at the cache (`TransactionProcessor._get_debit_base_amount`, which returns null without a rate, and `ledger_service.get_balances_at_date`, the cache less the active postings dated after it and the expenses still waiting for a value on that date, `posting_crud.get_sums_at_date`), so a back-dated expense from an account that is empty today is valued when the account had a rate on that date. An emptied account loses its rate, so an overdraft of such an account is unvalued until it is credited again; an overdraft of an account that keeps a rate is valued at it.

## Unvalued expenses

A new credit card in a foreign currency has no rate, and the cost of its spending in the base currency is known only when it is repaid. So an expense from an account without a rate is stored with a null `base_currency_amount` and null base amounts on both postings, and adds nothing to the account's `base_balance`. `ledger_service.get_valued_amount` reads a null base amount as 0 wherever base amounts are summed. The constraint trigger `transaction_valuation` (`app/models/accounting/transaction.py`, created by the revision `unvalued_postings`) keeps a transaction either fully valued or fully unvalued: a CHECK cannot read another table, so a deferred trigger on `transactions` and `postings` compares the header with its postings at commit.

The recalculation values them (**Recalculation**). An expense valued by a later credit keeps that credit's date in `transactions.valued_on`. A walk from a date starts no later than the account's first unvalued expense or the first one valued on that date or later (`posting_crud.get_first_revaluable_date`), so a credit dated after them reaches them, and deleting or changing the credit that valued them values them again. The walk treats both kinds as waiting: an expense debit that finds a rate at the running sums is valued at it like any debit and loses its `valued_on`; one that finds none waits, and the next credit that brings its own base amount, an adjustment excepted, values every waiting debit before it at that credit's rate (`get_base_amount` with the credit as the balance), oldest first, with their headers and the EXPENSE postings, and sets their `valued_on` to its date. Debits still waiting when the walk ends return to null. The rate at the running sums leaves the waiting debits out, as `get_balances_at_date` does, since their amounts are in the balance but not in the base value: an adjustment that gives such an account a rate values the next debit at the adjustment's own rate, not at the whole debt's.

The summary keeps them apart: earned and spent sum the valued amounts only, and `unvalued_spent` lists the unvalued expenses of the month as amounts in the currency of the debited account, one per currency in code order (`transaction_crud.get_unvalued_expense_sums`).

## Estimates

An unvalued amount has no base value until a credit values it, so the read responses carry an estimate at today's market rate next to it, computed on read and never stored: `estimated_base_currency_amount` on a transaction and `estimated_base_amount` on its postings (`transaction_service`, on list and get), `estimated_base_balance` on a money account with unvalued debits, its `base_currency_balance` plus those debits at the market rate (`account_service`, on list and get, over `posting_crud.get_unvalued_sums` and `account_service.get_unvalued_estimates`), and on the summary `estimated_total_balance`, the total plus the same unvalued debits at the market rate, and `estimated_unvalued_spent`, the month's `unvalued_spent` in the base currency. A transaction's estimate is its debit's amount at the rate of the debit's currency, and its credit takes the opposite, so the estimated base amounts sum to zero as stored ones do. A field is null when there is nothing to estimate or no rate for it. The responses of writes leave every estimate null, so no outbound call happens while a write's transaction holds its locks; the client reads the estimates from the lists it refetches. A summary total is null when any of its parts has no rate, so it is never a partial sum.

The rates come from `GET /v6/latest/USD` of open.er-api.com (`exchange_rate_settings.url`), a free endpoint without a key that updates once a day. `exchange_rate_service.get_base_currency_rates` keeps the USD rates in process memory for `cache_seconds` (12 hours) and derives the rate of every currency to the user's base currency from them; it is called only when a response has something to estimate, so a request without unvalued amounts never reaches the provider. A provider that fails, answers an error or sends rates `ExchangeRates` refuses is logged at WARNING and changes nothing: the rates cached last stay in use, and with none cached every estimate is null. The next attempt waits `RETRY_SECONDS` (5 minutes), and the attempt time is set before the request goes out, so concurrent requests at expiry make one call and the others answer from the cache. A user without a base currency gets no rates. The provider's terms ask for a link to it wherever its rates are shown, which is the frontend's to show next to an estimate. Tests point the URL at `open.er-api.test` and answer it through `httpx_mock`; the autouse `empty_exchange_rates_cache` fixture in `tests/conftest.py` empties the cache before each test.

## Exchange differences

A credit brings its own base amount, so repaying a debt at another rate than the one the debt was valued at would leave a base value on an account whose balance is 0: a EUR card at -600 EUR worth -660 USD repaid by 600 EUR bought for 650 USD would end at 0 EUR and -10 USD, off the summary total and with no rate. So when a credit that keeps its own base amount takes an account from a balance of 0 or less to 0 or more, the walk computes the exchange difference (`ledger_service.get_exchange_difference`): the base value the account should end with, 0 for a zero balance and otherwise the new balance at the credit's rate, less the running base value and the credit's base amount. A difference other than 0 is posted inside the credit's transaction as two postings of amount 0 in the account's currency: one on the account with the difference and one on the user's EQUITY account, Adjustments, with its opposite, so the base amounts still sum to zero and the header keeps the credit's amount. An adjustment keeps the rate and gets none. The walk keeps these postings current: it updates them when the difference changes, deletes them when it becomes 0 (`posting_crud.delete_by_ids`), creates them when one appears (reading the EQUITY account only then; a user without an active one answers 404 `ENTITY_NOT_FOUND`), and changes the account's `base_balance` cache by the change.

## Recalculation

Valuation follows the order of the transaction dates, not the order of entry. An account's postings are ordered by the transaction date, then by the transaction's `entry_order`, which a database sequence (`transactions_entry_order_seq`) gives on every create and again on every update, so it follows entry. After every create, update and delete the base processor calls `ledger_service.recalculate_accounts` with a start date per money account, which walks each of them over its active postings from that date: the transaction's date on create and delete (`ledger_service.get_money_account_dates`), and on update first the old money accounts from the old date and then the new ones from the new date. The walk starts from the cached sums less the stored postings it is about to walk, not from a sum of the earlier postings, so a cache with no postings behind it keeps its opening value. A credit keeps its base amount; a debit, and either posting of an adjustment on the account, since an adjustment keeps the rate, is valued again by `get_base_amount` at the running sums, and one that finds no rate there keeps its stored base amount, an expense excepted, which waits (**Unvalued expenses**), so a back-dated debit or the deletion of the credit that funded it is never refused. When such a base amount changes, the other posting and the header's `base_currency_amount` change with it, and a money account on the other side of a transfer gets the change in its cache and is walked in turn from the transfer's date. A receiving account in the base currency takes the new value as its amount too, since a transfer into the base currency derives that amount from the debit's base amount and an account in the base currency keeps `balance` equal to `base_balance`. A change only moves to postings later in date order, so the walk ends. A recalculation that leaves the cache of a deleted account changed answers 403 `DELETED_ACCOUNT_TRANSACTION_FORBIDDEN` with that account's id and is rolled back, as creating or deleting a transaction of a deleted account is: the user restores the account first. A deleted account outside the base currency that the walk empties again ends with an unchanged cache and passes. The writes go through `posting_crud.update_amounts`, `transaction_crud.update_base_currency_amounts`, `transaction_crud.update_valued_dates`, `posting_crud.create_batch` and `posting_crud.delete_by_ids` for exchange differences, and `account_crud.add_to_balance`, and the processor reads the transaction again afterwards, so its answer shows the recalculated amounts.

`create`, `update` and `delete` lock all of the user's money accounts in id order (`account_crud.lock_money_accounts`) before anything else, `update` and `delete` before they lock the transaction row and the processor before it locks its own accounts, whatever their status, since a walk can reach a deleted account through an old transfer. Every account a recalculation reaches is locked before any of them is read, and two requests of one user cannot deadlock or recalculate one account at once.

## Balance adjustments

An ADJUSTMENT transaction (`transaction_processor/adjustment.py`) changes an account's balance by the amount the user enters: the starting balance after onboarding, or the correction when the account drifts from the bank. It is created by `POST /accounting/accounts/{account_id}/adjustment` (`transaction_service.adjust_balance`), which answers the transaction. The EQUITY account answers 404 `ENTITY_NOT_FOUND`, as every other account call on it does. The body, `AdjustmentRequest`, carries `amount`, the signed change of the balance in the account's currency, and the service passes it with the path's account as `AdjustmentCreate` to the processor. It lives in `transaction_service` rather than `account_service`, because the processors import `user_service`, which imports `account_service`. The processor locks the account and posts the amount between the account and the user's EQUITY account, Adjustments: a positive amount credits the account and a negative one debits it. A user without an active EQUITY account answers 404 `ENTITY_NOT_FOUND`. An amount of zero is refused by the request validation of `AdjustmentRequest`. `base_currency_amount` is the amount valued by `ledger_service.get_base_amount` at the sums on the adjustment's date when the account is in the base currency or has a rate there, so an adjustment keeps the rate; an account outside the base currency without a rate takes it from the request, and answers 403 `NO_ACCOUNT_BASE_CURRENCY_RATE` without it. That also covers a decrease of an empty account below zero, such as the starting balance of a credit card, which then has a rate for its debts. A request that sends `base_currency_amount` for an account that has a rate answers 422 `BASE_CURRENCY_AMOUNT_NOT_ALLOWED` instead of having the value silently ignored. The not-found and deleted-account checks are the base processor's `_validate_account_is_active`, shared by the adjustment and the account validations of the other types. The currencies of an adjustment are the locked account's, so `create` checks no currency for it (`_get_request_currencies`), while the other types check the distinct currencies of the request before the processor reads any account. Adjustments are neither income nor expense: reports that sum incomes and expenses read only those two types. An adjustment is deleted like any other transaction, which moves the balance back.

`PUT /accounting/accounts/{account_id}/adjustment/{transaction_id}` (`transaction_service.update_adjustment`) takes the same body and runs the base processor's `update` (**Movements**), so the adjustment keeps its id, moves to the end of its date and its account is recalculated from the old date and then from the new one. Its amount, date and comment change; its account never does: `Adjustment._validate_update`, the hook `update` calls after the type check, answers 404 `ENTITY_NOT_FOUND` when the path's account is not the money account of the transaction's postings, the EQUITY account included. An adjustment body sent for a movement answers 422 `TRANSACTION_TYPE_CHANGE_NOT_ALLOWED`, and an adjustment whose account is deleted, the write-off of a deleted account included, 403 `DELETED_ACCOUNT_TRANSACTION_FORBIDDEN`. `base_currency_amount` follows the rule of a create, applied to the ledger without the transaction, which `update` has already reversed by then.

A client learns whether an adjustment on a date needs `base_currency_amount` from `GET /accounting/accounts/{account_id}?date=` (`account_service.get_account`): with a date, the `balance` and `base_balance` of the answer are the sums of `ledger_service.get_balances_at_date`, the ones the processor decides the rate by, so `base_currency_balance` and `base_currency_rate` follow them. They are not the balance the account had on that date, since the expenses still waiting for a value on it are left out. With `excluded_transaction_id` next to the date, the sums also leave that transaction's postings out (`posting_crud.get_sums_at_date`), so a client editing an adjustment asks about the ledger its update is decided by; the read only subtracts the postings and does not replay the recalculation the update runs after removing them. For an account outside the base currency a rate of 0 or less means the request must carry `base_currency_amount`; an account in the base currency never takes it. `estimated_base_balance` stays null with a date, since the estimate covers the unvalued expenses of every date. For the kinds that cache no balances the date changes nothing.

## Summary

An account's balance in the base currency is its cached `base_balance` rounded to cents. The account response exposes it as the computed `base_currency_balance`, and `GET /accounting/summary` (`summary_service.get_summary`) sums the same rounded values of the user's active ASSET and LIABILITY accounts as `total_balance`, so the total always equals the sum of the balances the client shows. The summary takes `month`, any date inside the month and today's month by default, and answers its first day. The backend works in UTC throughout, so that default is the UTC date; time zones belong to the frontend, which sends `month` when the user's local month differs. Earned and spent are the sums of `base_currency_amount` of the active income and expense transactions dated inside the month, read in one aggregate query, which skips unvalued expenses; left over is their difference and may be negative. `unvalued_spent` lists those expenses by currency (**Unvalued expenses**), and `estimated_total_balance` and `estimated_unvalued_spent` estimate what is left unvalued (**Estimates**).
